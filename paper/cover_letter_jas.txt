Dear Editor,

We are pleased to submit our manuscript entitled "Is the q-Weibull tail parameter identifiable under interval censoring? A diagnostic comparison with gamma frailty and a likelihood ratio test" for consideration in the Journal of Applied Statistics.

The q-Weibull distribution is increasingly used in reliability and survival analysis to model lifetime data with heavier-than-Weibull tails or bounded support. However, when data are interval-censored (as in periodic follow-up studies), the tail parameter q is poorly identified. We show that the q-Weibull model with q>1 is mathematically indistinguishable from the Weibull-gamma frailty model, which is a standard model for unobserved heterogeneity.

Our contributions are:
1. A mathematical analysis showing the two models belong to the same power-law family.
2. A Fisher information analysis quantifying the weak identification of q under interval censoring.
3. A likelihood ratio test with demonstrated size and power via simulation.
4. A practical diagnostic framework for practitioners.

We apply our methods to three real interval-censored datasets from dental research, epidemiology, and preclinical cancer research.

We believe this paper fits the scope of the Journal of Applied Statistics because it addresses a practical problem (model selection under interval censoring) with real data applications and provides actionable guidance for practitioners.

This manuscript has not been published elsewhere and is not under consideration by another journal. All authors have approved the submission.
