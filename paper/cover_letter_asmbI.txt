Dear Editor,

We are pleased to submit our manuscript entitled "Practical identifiability of the q-Weibull tail parameter in reliability and survival analysis: confounding with gamma frailty under interval censoring" for consideration in the Applied Stochastic Models in Business and Industry.

The q-Weibull distribution is increasingly used in reliability engineering to model lifetime data with heavier-than-Weibull tails or bounded support. However, when data are interval-censored (common in reliability testing and periodic inspection), the tail parameter q is poorly identified. We show that the q-Weibull model and the Weibull-gamma frailty model produce nearly identical survival curves over the observed range, making them practically indistinguishable.

Our contributions are:
1. A mathematical analysis showing the two models belong to the same power-law family.
2. A Fisher information analysis quantifying the weak identification of q under interval censoring.
3. A likelihood ratio test with demonstrated size and power.
4. A practical diagnostic framework for reliability practitioners.

We apply our methods to three real interval-censored datasets from dental research, epidemiology, and preclinical cancer research.

We believe this paper fits the scope of Applied Stochastic Models in Business and Industry because it addresses a practical problem in reliability engineering (model selection under interval censoring) with real data applications and provides actionable guidance for practitioners.

This manuscript has not been published elsewhere and is not under consideration by another journal. All authors have approved the submission.
